What Eat IQ is

Eat IQ estimates the nutrition in food from photos, barcodes, and descriptions, and keeps a log of what you have eaten.

Estimates are estimates

Every number Eat IQ shows you is an estimate, not a measurement. Photo scans infer portion size from what is visible in the frame; database entries describe a typical version of a food, not the one on your plate. Real meals vary widely. Treat the numbers as a useful guide and correct them when you know better — every value in the app is editable.

Not medical advice

Eat IQ is a general wellness and fitness tool. It is not a medical device, and nothing in it is medical, nutritional, or dietary advice, diagnosis, or treatment. Calorie and macro targets are calculated from standard formulas applied to the details you enter — they are a starting point, not a prescription. Talk to a doctor or a registered dietitian before making significant changes to your diet, and especially if you are pregnant, under 18, managing a medical condition such as diabetes or kidney disease, or have a history of disordered eating. Never disregard professional advice because of something this app displayed.

The novelty scanner

Eat IQ will cheerfully estimate the "nutrition" of things that are not food, such as your hand or your cat. This is a joke. It is clearly labelled as such in the app, the numbers are meaningless, and it is not a suggestion that you eat anything. Do not eat anything that is not food.
